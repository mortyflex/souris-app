# Souris — Project Instructions

## 1. Product

Souris is a lightweight native business and appointment management application for beauty professionals.

The first real-world implementation and validation context is:

- a hairdresser;
- a single professional;
- mobile-first daily usage.

However, Souris is NOT a hairdressing-only product.

The core domain must remain suitable for beauty professionals such as:

- hairdressers;
- nail technicians;
- estheticians;
- lash and brow professionals;
- other appointment-based beauty professionals.

The initial user experience may be optimized for hairdressing when this improves usability.

The core domain must not permanently couple Souris to hair salons.

Product principle:

> Generic in the data model, specific in the user experience.

---

## 2. Product Mission

Souris exists to give a beauty professional the minimum set of tools required to manage their daily business efficiently without the complexity of traditional salon-management software.

The application should feel:

- simple;
- fast;
- clear;
- native;
- warm;
- professional;
- pleasant to use repeatedly throughout the day.

Souris should not become an ERP.

Souris should not reproduce every capability found in large salon-management platforms.

Prefer a small number of excellent workflows over a large number of mediocre ones.

---

## 3. Product Simplicity

Souris competes on simplicity.

Do not add a capability merely because another salon-management application has it.

A new feature should normally answer at least one of these questions:

- Does it make appointments easier to manage?
- Does it make client follow-up genuinely useful?
- Does it make services and processing time easier to understand?
- Does it make product retail or stock easier to manage?
- Does it remove a repetitive daily task?
- Does it materially improve the professional's working day?

If not, it is probably outside the core product.

Do not introduce speculative product complexity.

# 8. Architecture Philosophy

Souris uses a pragmatic feature-first architecture with an explicit framework-independent domain layer.

Target direction:

```text

├── domain/
├── features/
├── shared/
│   ├── ui/
│   ├── icons/
```

# 11. Appointments Are the Core Domain

Appointments are the most important business concept in Souris.

Never simplify an appointment to:

```text
start + duration
```

A Souris appointment is composed of ordered service items.

Each item contains a snapshot of the service selected at the time of booking.

Each item contains ordered phases.

Conceptually:

```text
Appointment
├── id
├── businessId
├── clientId
├── staffMemberId
├── startAt
├── status
└── items[]
    ├── AppointmentItem
    │   ├── serviceId
    │   ├── serviceOptionId?
    │   ├── order
    │   ├── serviceName
    │   ├── serviceType
    │   ├── price
    │   └── phases[]
    │       ├── name
    │       ├── durationMinutes
    │       └── requiresStaff
    └── ...
```

Exact implementation types will be introduced deliberately in the domain phase.

Do not invent them prematurely in UI code.

---

# 12. Service Types

The scheduling domain distinguishes:

```text
SERVICE
TECHNIQUE
```

## SERVICE

A SERVICE is a simple professional service.

Examples:

- haircut;
- blow-dry;
- simple treatment.

A SERVICE requires the professional during its duration.

A SERVICE does NOT contain processing / unattended time.

---

## TECHNIQUE

A TECHNIQUE may contain multiple ordered phases.

Example:

```text
Application
15 min
requiresStaff = true

Processing
35 min
requiresStaff = false
```

A TECHNIQUE may also follow:

```text
active
→ processing
→ active
```

A TECHNIQUE may contain:

- processing time;
- zero processing time;
- multiple active phases;
- multiple ordered phases.

Never hardcode:

```text
TECHNIQUE = application + processing
```

The real abstraction is:

```text
TECHNIQUE = ordered phases
```

---

# 13. Processing Time

Processing time is a core Souris concept.

A phase where:

```text
requiresStaff = false
```

means:

- the client is still in the appointment;
- the appointment is still running;
- the professional is not required during that phase.

This may allow the professional to work with another client.

Examples include beauty workflows where a product, treatment, color, or other process must remain in place for some time.

Do not couple processing time specifically to hair coloring.

The concept must remain generic enough for other beauty professions.

---

# 14. Appointment Timeline

Appointment items are ordered.

Appointment phases are ordered.

The appointment timeline is calculated from:

```text
appointment start
+
ordered items
+
ordered phases
```

Reordering service items recalculates the timeline.

The timeline must be calculated by domain logic rather than visual components.

UI components consume the calculated timeline.

They must not independently invent timing rules.

---

# 15. Appointment Snapshots

Appointments must preserve historical reality.

When a service is added to an appointment, the appointment must eventually preserve the relevant service information as a snapshot.

If the catalog later changes:

- service name;
- price;
- duration;
- phases;

an existing historical appointment must not silently change.

Catalog configuration and appointment history are different concerns.

---

# 16. Appointment Overlap

Multiple appointments at the same time are allowed.

This is an explicit product rule.

The professional may intentionally handle multiple clients simultaneously.

Therefore:

- appointment creation must not automatically fail because another appointment overlaps;
- appointment movement must not automatically fail because another appointment overlaps.

Overlap detection may later support:

- information;
- warnings;
- suggestions;
- availability calculations;
- multi-staff capabilities.

But overlap is currently NON-BLOCKING for professional scheduling.

Do not reintroduce conflict blocking without an explicit product decision.

---

# 17. Manual Scheduling vs Future Online Booking

Manual professional scheduling and future customer self-booking are different concerns.

They must not share one simplistic definition of availability.

## Manual professional scheduling

The professional controls their own agenda.

They may intentionally:

- overlap appointments;
- work with another client during processing time;
- accept unusual scheduling arrangements.

Overlaps are therefore non-blocking.

---

## Future customer self-booking

Future customers may eventually book appointments remotely.

Souris must then expose only appointment slots considered bookable under a dedicated availability policy.

Do NOT assume:

```text
"the professional is technically allowed to create this appointment"
```

means:

```text
"this slot should be offered to a customer online"
```

Future online-booking availability may consider:

- business working hours;
- staff working hours;
- breaks;
- time off;
- existing appointments;
- service phases;
- processing phases;
- configurable concurrency rules;
- booking lead time;
- booking horizon;
- blocked periods;
- service availability;
- staff availability.

These rules are FUTURE scope.

Do not build an online booking engine until explicitly requested.

Design the domain so that an availability layer can be added later without rewriting the Appointment model.

# 19. Appointment Lifecycle

Appointment statuses may include:

```text
SCHEDULED
CONFIRMED
IN_PROGRESS
COMPLETED
CANCELLED
NO_SHOW
```

Cancellation and no-show are historical outcomes.

They must not delete the appointment.

A cancellation may eventually contain:

```text
cancelledAt
cancelledBy:
  CLIENT
  BUSINESS
reason?
```

A no-show may contain:

```text
recordedAt
```

Permanent deletion is different.

Deletion should only be used for things such as:

- incorrect entry;
- duplicate;
- appointment accidentally created.

Do not treat:

- cancellation;
- no-show;
- deletion

as equivalent actions.
